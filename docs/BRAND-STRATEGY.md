# Kulha! brand and conversion strategy

## Positioning

**Kulha! is a Moroccan grocery-ordering brand connecting people with nearby shops and markets, with home delivery depending on service coverage.** The distinction is not a vague promise to be faster or cheaper; it is a local-first shopping experience grounded in the everyday Moroccan market and neighbourhood-shop context.

### Five-second value proposition

“Order everyday groceries from nearby shops and markets. Kulha! brings your basket to your door when your area is covered.”

French lead: “Faites vos courses auprès des commerces et marchés de proximité. Kulha! vous aide à commander vos essentiels du quotidien et à les recevoir chez vous.”

Darija lead: “طلب قضايات الدار من الحوانت والأسواق اللي قراب ليك. Kulha! كتعاونك تجمع طلبك وتوصلك القضايات حتى لدارك حسب المنطقة.”

## Audience and objections

- Busy households: can I get routine essentials without visiting several shops? Explain browse → basket → confirm → receive.
- Young professionals: can I place an order around a busy day? Do not promise a time until the zone's delivery SLA is confirmed.
- Students: can I see the full cost first? Show all fees and the estimate before final confirmation.
- Shop owners: will the platform represent my shop fairly? Publish commercial terms, commissions, eligibility and logistics only after approval.

Objection handling on the page:

- **Price:** make product prices, delivery fees and minimum basket visible before confirmation. Actual amounts remain a launch dependency.
- **Freshness / quality:** explain which shop prepares the order and publish real selection, handling and substitution standards once agreed. No blanket freshness guarantee yet.
- **Delivery time:** show the estimated window for the specific order and area; no generic “in minutes” claim.
- **Trust:** provide the actual operator identity, working support contacts, legal terms, payment details, refund steps and a clear order summary before launch.
- **Why not go to the souk?** Kulha! is an additional option for days when ordering from home is more convenient; it does not position the market as obsolete.

## Trust plan

Trust should be built from operational clarity, not borrowed credibility. No partner logos, ratings, testimonials, press logos, awards or stats are shown. Three hidden `FUTURE` slots are included in the home component for later use only after the brand obtains verifiable assets.

## Early-stage note

The team note is deliberately transparent: Kulha! is in development. There is no waitlist, launch discount or early-adopter claim until the business confirms one.

## Brand name: Kulha!

The spelling is short, memorable and food-adjacent, but it is not self-explanatory. In Moroccan Arabic, a nearby form **kolha / kollha** can mean “all of it” in feminine agreement; Darija spelling and transliteration vary. A listener may also associate the sound with **koul** (“eat”) and the object pronoun **ha**, giving a playful food-related reading, but this should be checked with native speakers rather than treated as the single literal meaning. Sources note that Darija transliteration has no single official standard and that forms vary by region. [Darija Dictionary guide](https://darijadictionary.com/en/guide/) · [Sa9si: kollo / kollha](https://www.sa9si.com/fr/marocain/kollo/) · [Tajine Qui Parle: kolha](https://en.tajinequiparle.com/english-moroccan-arabic/all/)

**Recommendation:** keep the exclamation mark and the brand spelling as supplied. It has warmth and memorability for a grocery brand. Before a major paid campaign, test pronunciation and meaning with speakers from Casablanca, Rabat, Fès, Marrakech, the North and Amazigh-speaking communities. Check trademark, domain and app-store availability separately. No claim is made here that the name is legally cleared or safe across every Amazigh language variety.

## Visual direction

- Deep green #073324 and cream #FCF4E7 as the anchor; yellow #FDBE2E for action; tomato #EE3F29 and leaf #7EC34A as small accents.
- Nunito 800–1000 for headlines, 700–800 for controls, 600–700 for body. Noto Sans Arabic is included as an Arabic fallback.
- Moroccan cues should come from material, colour, market produce, shopfront geometry and Darija language—not costume clichés, stock souk collages or decorative stereotypes.
